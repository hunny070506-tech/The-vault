Ramayana: Part 1 Theatrical Review

3/5 · One-Time Watch

A visually grand magnum opus anchored by Yash's commanding Ravana and Oscar-tier sound design, held back by an underwhelming score and an emotional core that never hits as hard as its frames.

Producer Namit Malhotra fulfills his promise of mounting the Ramayana on a genuine global canvas, backed by Nitesh Tiwari’s assured presentation. Visually, the film operates at the top of its game—barring a few cartoonish CG demon exceptions—and the cinematography is striking even when occasional staging and blocking choices could have been tighter. Complementing the visuals is world-class sound design built to the standard of Oscar-winning spectacles.

Performance-wise, Ranbir Kapoor, Sai Pallavi, and Ravie Dubey don't merely play their parts; they inhabit and live these characters, though Shatrughna is completely ignored in the narrative. Yet it is Yash who rules the film as Ravana with a magnificent, towering screen presence. Tiwari’s sharpest directorial innovation lies in how he stages Ravana’s internal psychological battles and grounds the primary characters' motivations. The action choreography is hit-and-miss: early skirmishes featuring Ram and Lakshman cutting through demons work well, and the Jatayu sequence stands out as the film's undisputed, masterfully choreographed highlight, whereas the larger set pieces meant to showcase god-level powers needed far better execution.

Where the film stumbles is its pacing and emotional resonance. Paradoxically, the major plot turns that set the Ramayana in motion feel rushed, even as the lengthy runtime isn't consistently engaging. Paired with a Hans Zimmer and A.R. Rahman score that could have been way better, Ramayana: Part 1 remains a decent classic magnum opus that needed its emotional charge to match the height of its visuals.
